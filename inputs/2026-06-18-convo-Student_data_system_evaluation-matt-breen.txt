Student data system evaluation
==============================
Date: June 18, 2026
Time: 8:51 AM – 10:07 AM CDT
Location: 5621 CR-101, Minnetonka, MN 55345


OVERVIEW
--------
Marley met with a school district data/assessment administrator to discuss the district's educational data systems, attendance tracking challenges, and potential technology solutions. The administrator walked through their current workflows using Skyward, EduClimber, and various manual processes for tracking attendance, interventions, and new student data onboarding. They explored Edia as a potential solution for attendance communication and intervention management, and discussed broader challenges with EdTech costs, data integration, and building teacher engagement with analytics tools.


ATMOSPHERE
----------
Collaborative and exploratory — both parties were openly troubleshooting system limitations, sharing frustrations with manual workarounds, and brainstorming improvements. There's mutual respect for the complexity of district data needs and a pragmatic, "build scrappy first" mindset.


KEY TAKEAWAYS
-------------
  - Attendance tracking is a major pain point: The district lacks CIS-reported rate data in EduClimber, forcing manual workarounds. Full-day rates are misleading when tardies count against them, and period-level tracking is inconsistently implemented across buildings.
  - High school has the most advanced (but still flawed) system: A clerical staffer built a color-coded dashboard with alert thresholds (4, 7, 10 unexcused periods), but it's described as "not what you'd want to carry into the mid-21st century."
  - Edia is under evaluation for attendance communication: The administrator watched Edia's testimonial and likes the proactive messaging, reply tracking, and counselor alert features. Marley raised concerns about whether it handles the full intervention workflow when families don't respond. Cost is unknown but suspected to be high.
  - New student data onboarding is broken: Only ~65 of ~200 expected new families completed a data collection form. Incoming student records often live as attachments in Skyward profiles, requiring manual escalation when teachers raise concerns. The district is piloting summer reading assessments for data gaps.
  - EduClimber usage is growing but uneven: The district pays $71K–$80K for 11,000 licenses but estimates only using $30K–$45K worth of functionality. Renewed FastBridge for 3 years, EduClimber for only 1 year due to leadership skepticism.
  - Marley's cost sensitivity: Marley noted that in EdTech, "anything over $5 per pupil is a no-go" for many districts, and referenced prior experience with Infinite Campus pricing discussions.
  - Data request bottlenecks: Hardest internal requests involve incomplete data profiles (new students, multilingual learners, longitudinal tracking). The district lacks good systems for multi-year student tracking, especially when students leave and re-enroll.
  - Teacher engagement with data is a cultural challenge: The administrator wants teachers to review individual student growth trajectories and pass context to next year's teachers, but this isn't currently an expectation. There's interest in clustering students by growth patterns (coasters, stars, climbers, etc.) to make data more actionable.
  - NWEA MAP preferred over FastBridge: The administrator values MAP's conditional growth index and district-normed comparisons, despite its $14.50/pupil cost. FastBridge is distrusted for growth metrics, though used for reading/math screeners.
