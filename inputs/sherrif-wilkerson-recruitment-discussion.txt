## Summary

Marley conducted an informational interview with a Hennepin County Sheriff's Office recruitment and public affairs staffer (Deputy Wilkerson, ~30 years in law enforcement, previously Dakota County, now Hennepin County for 2 years). The interview explored operational pain points in law enforcement recruitment, particularly around pipeline management, funnel breakage, and technology gaps. The conversation was arranged after Marley spoke with the Sheriff about problems to potentially solve in the space.

## Atmosphere

Warm, candid, and somewhat meandering — the interviewee was open and emotionally expressive, sharing personal stories about PTSD, partner loss, and the stresses of the job. There was mutual respect and a shared appreciation for problem-solving, though technical difficulties (laptop camera issues) caused some interruption at the start.

## Key Take aways

- **Recruitment pipeline is fragmented**: Applications flow through "Big County" (Hennepin County central HR), creating delays and miscommunication. The Sheriff's Office lacks direct control over initial candidate screening and routing.
- **Major funnel breakage in middle stages**: Deputy Wilkerson brings candidates in through events and outreach, but loses visibility once they apply online. No system exists to pass her qualitative notes ("soft ratings," impressions) to professional standards/background investigators.
- **Unconventional recruitment works**: They successfully hired 90 people after two mall hiring fairs (Mall of America, Ridgedale) with on-site applications and interviews — a break from traditional law enforcement recruitment channels.
- **Military recruitment is a priority**: Active outreach to MACV and military personnel, but same handoff problems exist — names collected at events don't integrate well with downstream processes.
- **Retention is deeply tied to job stress/PTSD**: The interviewee described a "revolving door," with officers burning out from 24/7 immersion in the job. Shared a tragic story of a partner who suppressed trauma and later died from alcohol-related causes.
- **Technology pain points are acute**: Frustration with new systems, poor integration between county-level and department-level tools, and a recent forced migration from Guardian Tracking to Axon for performance/records management.
- **Public affairs coordination is chaotic**: Multiple divisions (Water Patrol, Mounted Horse, COPA, VSD) receive event requests through ad-hoc channels. They just started a single email inbox to triage, but lack clear ownership/assignment workflows.
- **Target hiring volume**: ~40-50 per year currently, though was higher (90) during a one-time push to meet jail staffing mandates. Many retirements expected in next 2 years.
- **Competition between departments**: Deputy Wilkerson takes a collaborative approach — refers candidates to other departments if not a fit, viewing "one person in uniform anywhere" as a win.
- **Personal connection discovered**: Marley's grandfather (Norm Hansen) was an Apple Valley officer who retired around when Deputy Wilkerson started in 1996/97.
