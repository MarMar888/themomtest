## Stage: Problem Validation

### Operations & Inventory Pain
*(from money_questions.txt — approved May 12, 2026)*

**Q-OI-01** | `[problem-validation] [ops] [inventory]`
"Walk me through what happened the last time you ran out of a popular model before the end of season."
*Why: Forces a specific story. Reveals whether inventory mismatch is a real recurring pain or mild friction.*

**Q-OI-02** | `[problem-validation] [ops] [inventory] [workflow]`
"What does your process look like right now for tracking what's selling fast vs. sitting on the floor?"
*Why: Reveals current workflow and workarounds. "We kind of just watch it" = real gap.*

**Q-OI-03** | `[problem-validation] [ops] [have-they-searched]`
"Have you ever tried any software or tools to help manage inventory or forecasting? What happened?"
*Why: The have-they-searched test. No → problem isn't painful enough. Yes → learn what failed and why.*

**Q-OI-04** | `[problem-validation] [ops] [inventory] [past-specific]`
"What's the worst inventory mistake that happened in the last year or two — over-ordered, under-ordered, wrong mix?"
*Why: Past-specific, emotional. Reveals actual cost of the problem.*

**Q-OI-05** | `[problem-validation] [ops] [decision-making] [past-specific]`
"When you placed your January order, how did you decide what quantities to order? Walk me through it."
*Why: Exposes the actual decision process. Reveals where judgment calls happen and where they're flying blind.*

**Q-OI-06** | `[problem-validation] [ops] [inventory] [cash-flow] [retail] [past-specific]`
"Walk me through what actually happens to your cash position at the end of a season where you've over-ordered — how does it play out?"
*Why: Moves past the ordering mistake to the downstream pain. Cash exposure is the real problem, not the bad call itself — confirmed by Something Safari (May 2026).*

**Q-OI-07** | `[problem-validation] [ops] [inventory] [workarounds] [retail] [past-specific]`
"When you've made a bad inventory call before, how did you actually dig out of it?"
*Why: Reveals recovery behavior and workarounds. Whether they have a real process or just absorb the loss tells you how serious the problem is.*

### Customer & Sales Dynamics
*(from money_questions.txt — approved May 12, 2026)*

**Q-CS-01** | `[problem-validation] [sales] [past-specific]`
"Tell me about the last time a customer walked out because you didn't have what they were looking for."
*Why: Puts a real story behind lost sales. Reveals frequency and emotional weight.*

**Q-CS-02** | `[problem-validation] [sales] [past-specific]`
"What do customers get frustrated about most when they come in? Give me the last specific example you can remember."
*Why: Past-specific. Surfaces real pain from the customer side the owner has to manage.*

**Q-CS-03** | `[problem-validation] [sales] [past-specific] [workflow]`
"You mentioned people come in not knowing what they want — walk me through what that looks like on a busy Saturday."
*Why: Anchors the general statement to a real moment. Reveals actual cost in time and conversion.*

### Business Model & Costs
*(from money_questions.txt — approved May 12, 2026)*

**Q-BM-01** | `[problem-validation] [costs] [past-specific]`
"What's the single thing about running this shop that costs the most in time or money that you didn't expect when you started?"
*Why: Open-ended and past-oriented. Surfaces surprises — the real pain is often not what you assumed.*

**Q-BM-02** | `[problem-validation] [costs] [cash-flow] [past-specific]`
"When you hit that net 180 deadline in late summer and you haven't sold through — what actually happens? Walk me through a real example."
*Why: Cash flow crisis moment. Reveals severity of end-of-season problem and what they actually do.*

**Q-BM-03** | `[problem-validation] [costs] [ops]`
"How much time does the owner spend on things that aren't biking — admin, ordering, accounting, staffing? Is that a sore spot?"
*Why: Probes whether operational overhead is a real pain the owner feels, or just cost of doing business.*

### Decision-Making Structure
*(from money_questions.txt — approved May 12, 2026)*

**Q-DM-01** | `[problem-validation] [decision-making]`
"If you were going to try a new tool or process here, who would have to sign off on that?"
*Why: Reveals the actual decision-maker. If it's the regional director and we've been talking to floor staff, wrong conversation.*

**Q-DM-02** | `[problem-validation] [decision-making] [spending] [past-specific]`
"Has the owner ever tried to buy something — software, a system, a service — to solve an operations problem and had it not work out? What happened?"
*Why: Reveals prior purchase behavior. One of the strongest signals of willingness to pay.*

### Loss Prevention / Shoplifting
*(approved May 18, 2026)*

**Q-SL-01** | `[problem-validation] [loss-prevention] [have-they-searched] [retail]`
"Have you ever tried any tools, cameras, or systems specifically to deal with shoplifting? What happened with those?"
*Why: The have-they-searched test. No → they've accepted it. Yes → learn what failed and why.*

**Q-SL-02** | `[problem-validation] [loss-prevention] [severity] [spending] [retail]`
"What does shoplifting actually cost you in a typical month — has anyone ever tried to put a number on it?"
*Why: Anchors vague frustration to real cost data. Whether they've quantified it signals how seriously they take it.*

**Q-SL-03** | `[problem-validation] [loss-prevention] [past-specific] [emotional-signal] [retail]`
"Walk me through the last time a shoplifting incident was really frustrating — what exactly happened, start to finish?"
*Why: Past-specific. Forces a full narrative instead of general frustration. Reveals the real operational workflow and pain.*

## Commitment-Closing Questions
*(from money_questions.txt — approved May 12, 2026)*

**Q-CC-01** | `[commitment] [intro-request]`
"Is there anyone else you'd recommend we talk to who deals with this stuff more directly than you do?"
*Why: Intro request = commitment signal. Enthusiasm → conversation was real. Deflection → probably wasn't.*

**Q-CC-02** | `[commitment] [time]`
"Would it be useful to show you what we're thinking about building and get your honest reaction? We could come back next week."
*Why: Asks for time commitment. Yes → real interest. "Let us know when you have something" → zombie.*

## The Destroy-Your-Business Question
*(from money_questions.txt — approved May 12, 2026)*

**Q-DYB-01** | `[destroy-your-business] [severity]`
"Is this actually a problem that bothers you enough that you'd spend money to fix it — or is it just something you've accepted as part of the job?"
*Why: Forces honest self-assessment. "Honestly, we've just accepted it" = most valuable signal you can get.*
