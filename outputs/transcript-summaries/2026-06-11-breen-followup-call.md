# Conversation Summary — Matt Breen (Father), Minnetonka District — Follow-up Debrief Call
**Date:** 2026-06-11 | **Format:** Phone call | **Interviewer:** Marley

---

## Who He Is

Matt Breen — Lead Data Scientist, Minnetonka School District. Marley's father. His wife is Christine Breen (Executive Director of Students), who is taking over attendance oversight on July 1. Annalise Peterson (attendance coordinator) reports under Christine. This is a follow-up to the Breen text chain (entry #9) and follows Marley's direct call with Anelise Peterson (entry #10).

---

## What He Said

**On the district's current plan:**
The plan is to implement weekly or bi-weekly SARB (Student Attendance Review Board) meetings at every elementary school — bringing together counselors, social workers, and administrators to review cases and develop intervention plans. The plan is "still that ambiguous" — there's a framework but not a systematic process yet across buildings. EduClimber will be used to surface the highest-priority cases, but EduClimber is not the driver: "we would move forward with or without EduClimber." The existing approach is reactive; the goal is to become proactive.

**On the attendance lead — personnel shift:**
The current person who technically oversees attendance is described as "pen and paper" — not strategic. Her role is shifting July 1 to report under Christine, who is more data/systems-oriented. Christine's oversight change is expected to increase urgency and openness to systematic solutions: "it just puts us in a position to be more receptive to what we can do to move it forward."

**On EduClimber's role and limitations:**
EduClimber is a data warehouse, not an SIS — Skyward is the SIS. EduClimber stores student data profiles, supports smart forms, and powers data walls. Guardian emails are NOT currently in EduClimber: "we don't even have Guardian emails in EduClimber right now because we haven't built a system that's needed that." EduClimber "cannot do everything that we want for attendance even at its best" but has family communication opportunities the district hasn't utilized. The new attendance features launching July 1 are unclear in scope — the father needs to evaluate them over the summer.

**On the two distinct problems:**
Validated Marley's framing that there are two separate problems: (1) systematic case management for highly truant students — a process problem requiring SARB meeting infrastructure and clear intervention ownership; (2) day-to-day agility — proactive, early communication with families via text versus end-of-day automated calls. Father agreed: "there's really a two-fold problem here. There's a systematic problem, and then there's an efficiency, agile, proactive."

**On timeliness as the core failure:**
"Did the student technically reach, say, seven absences three weeks ago, and we're just meeting on it now? And since then, they've had four more absences." The intervention is consistently late — not because the data doesn't exist, but because the process doesn't move fast enough.

**On EDIA:**
Marley introduced EDIA as a reference point. Father's response: interested but not urgent. He acknowledged that districts at the forefront might want custom business logic — e.g., recognizing that a student missing a week and a half three weeks into the school year is a different signal than missing a week and a half in April. "Some kind of stronger custom business logic that this might not have out of the box." He said he'd be curious to learn more but didn't request a demo or express urgency.

**On why COVID matters:**
COVID "punched a hole" in attendance culture — made families more comfortable with absences, and made districts more willing to address long-standing gaps. This is a window of organizational urgency that didn't exist pre-pandemic.

**On family communication:**
Families are more likely to respond to text than email or letters. Some communications are legally required in hard copy. End-of-day automated calls are culturally expected but annoying. The right channel isn't resolved.

- Guardian contact information is NOT in EduClimber — the district would have to build that infrastructure before any communication-layer tool could reach families through EduClimber
- The pen-and-paper attendance lead is being displaced by Christine on July 1 — a real organizational inflection point that could accelerate vendor evaluation
- "With or without EduClimber" is a meaningful signal: the district is process-first, not platform-dependent. External tools are not being ruled out, just not yet sought
- Timeliness is the gap — intervention consistently triggers weeks after the threshold is crossed
- The two-problem framing (systematic + agility) was confirmed by an insider, not just Marley's synthesis; the problems require different solutions and may have different buyers

## The Verdict

Three conversations in this space now — Breen text chain, Anelise Peterson, this call — all pointing at the same two-layer problem. The signal is strengthening on the process side (systematic intervention is underdeveloped, personnel shift is creating urgency, timeliness is the named failure). But the buy signal is still weak: the district is solving in-house, EduClimber is the default assumption, no budget discussion has happened. The test is whether other districts have the same problem and the same in-house-first instinct — or whether Minnetonka's approach is the exception. If other districts are in the same position and none have bought anything purpose-built, there may be a market. If Minnetonka is the outlier, the assumption needs to be revisited.
