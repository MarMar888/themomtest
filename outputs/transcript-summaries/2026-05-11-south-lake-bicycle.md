## Real Data

### Confirmed Problems

**Sunday chaos is real and unpredictable.** Aaron described Sundays as a "choose your adventure day" with no planning possible: it can be dead or overwhelmed with walk-ins wanting test rides, emergency repairs, and new purchases all at once. He named himself the Sunday guy because of his wrenching experience since 2016. Repair queue does not get touched on Sundays -- it accumulates and gets cleared Wednesday through Friday when walk-in traffic drops.

**Net 180 cash crunch is structural.** All independent bike shops operate on net 180 payment terms: they order 80% of inventory in January/February, receive it March/April, and do not pay until late summer. If the summer sell-through is weak, end-of-season fire sales begin in late August and September to hit the payment deadline. This is not a Marley/Lucas observation -- Aaron described the mechanics unprompted and with precision.

**Glut of unassembled bikes at season start.** When the spring delivery arrives, the shop receives hundreds of bikes at once. They need enough skilled wrenches to build them all before the season begins. This is a labor-coordination problem: too many bikes, narrow window, limited staff.

### Workarounds

- Aaron is the designated Sunday person precisely because unpredictability requires someone experienced enough to triage on the fly.
- Slower weekdays (Wednesday through Friday) are used to clear the repair backlog built up on Sundays.
- Used bikes that can't be saved are donated (bikes for kids programs or similar).

### Spending / Technology Investments

- None mentioned. The shop runs on direct rep experience and Aaron's 10-year wrenching background. No tools or software referenced.

### Solution-Seeking

- None confirmed. Aaron did not mention looking for any software, tool, or service to address inventory, Sunday operations, or the spring build-out crunch.

### Emotional Signals

- Genuine enthusiasm about the wrenching work ("this is more fun -- I'm building this bike from scratch"). Not frustrated with the job itself.
- "Sundays are just like, oh my god, so goddamn busy." Real emotional weight on Sunday unpredictability.
- "We never plan for anything on Sunday." Stated as accepted reality, not active pain to be solved.
