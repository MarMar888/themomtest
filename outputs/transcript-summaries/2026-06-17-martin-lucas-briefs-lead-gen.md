## Real Data

**Volume absorbs duds.** Martin's mental model is a pure volume play. "It's such a volume play that you only notice the wins." Contact accuracy and lead quality are not felt as pain because volume absorbs the cost of bad leads. This is a relevant negative signal for any product that sells on quality-over-quantity.

**Known contact accuracy friction exists but is tolerated.** "Sometimes on call lists it'll be the wrong person, or they retired." Martin acknowledges stale contact data as a real occurrence but treats it as normal friction, not a problem worth solving.

**Response rate is the real bottleneck, not lead quality.** "You still need them to answer the phone / respond to an email." Martin named conversion, not sourcing, as the hard part. Briefs addresses the sourcing side; his actual pain is on the conversion side.

**Manual AP process is the norm in smaller orgs.** "Most smaller orgs just have a pretty manual process when it comes to uploading invoices and expenses into their accounting software." In construction specifically, some use Bill.com but still without full automation.

**Manual companies are easier to convert than Bill.com users.** Confirmed directly: manual process companies are easier to sell into than those already using a competing tool. New domain companies are likely to be manual (just starting up), which is a positive signal for the Briefs targeting thesis.

**New/small companies are not Ramp's primary segment.** "Ramp sells to smaller companies and newer ones but it's not our bread and butter." Martin confirmed that the segment Briefs would surface is not Ramp's core focus. This limits Martin's usefulness as a validator of the thesis.

## The Verdict

Martin is a type 2 customer from momtest2-notes: he perceives the lead quality issue as theoretically worth solving, but it's too far down his priority list (volume works) to invest effort in solving. His actual pain is response rate, which Briefs does not address. The most useful data from this conversation is the manual-easier-to-convert signal, which indirectly supports the new domain thesis. The empirical test (give Martin a batch of new domain construction leads and measure conversion vs. his baseline) is the only remaining question this conversation cannot answer.

**Product-market fit note:** Briefs gets company phone numbers, not owner-level contacts. For Martin's use case, this means Briefs surfaces a company earlier but leaves the contact-finding problem unsolved -- the same problem he already has with stale call lists.
