## Real Data

### Confirmed Signals

**Acute staffing crisis is the named #1 pain.** Water Patrol operates with 9 full-time deputies (down from goal of 12). Often only 1.5 people working a shift (one deputy + sergeant splitting between days/nights). Volunteer program down from ~50 (15 years ago) to ~5. First year ever they got zero summer staffing pulled from the jail (their traditional pipeline).

**Tech budget already maxed.** Top-tier drones, sonar, GPS, radio. *"We have all the top-of-the-line"* equipment. Hardware is not the gap.

**Type 2 customer signal: doesn't believe software solves it.** After Marley revealed the software-discovery angle, the lieutenant's response was *"unless you can make a robot to do the job."* He named back-office admin as a possible software opportunity but with low specificity: *"makes reports quicker, makes arrest quicker."* This is the only direct software-positive signal in the conversation and it's narrow.

**Recruitment pipeline collapse is structural, not local.** *"The colleges overall, people are not taking the law enforcement classes."* Cites the George Floyd riots and ongoing ICE protests as the inflection point. Pipeline problem operates at the national level, not solvable by a single-county tool.

**Cross-agency dependency is the operational workaround.** Police agencies don't have boats. Fire is their backup on the water. They share attack channels and train together. This is operational structure, not a pain, but worth knowing for any future product.

**Hennepin scope is broader than Lake Minnetonka.** Water Patrol investigates every drowning in the county, including hotel pools. *"There's probably 10 drownings a year in hotel pools in Minnesota and Hennepin County."* This dropped as a side fact and was never explored.

### Workarounds

- Sergeant splits shifts (half days, half nights) to maintain minimum coverage.
- Lieutenant counts himself in the staffing math but *"realistically, I don't count"* because he's also doing leadership.
- Fire department covers on-water response when Water Patrol is committed elsewhere.

### Spending / Technology Investments

- Hardware: drones, sonar, GPS, radio. All described as top-tier and adequately funded.
- Software: not surfaced. *"I'm not a techie guy, I'm terrible at that."*

### Solution-Seeking

- Lieutenant has formally requested additional headcount from the sheriff annually. Denied due to broader county short-staffing (jail and courts also short).
- No tech procurement attempted for staffing or recruitment.

### Emotional Signals

- Resigned frustration on staffing: *"staffing is our biggest crutch, it's always our crutch."*
- Active discouragement of his own son entering law enforcement: *"the only thing I told him is don't be a cop because the way the public treats the police now."*
- Pride in equipment and team capability.

## The Verdict

Same disqualification shape as K-12, reached faster:

1. **Type 2 customer:** named the pain (staffing) but doesn't believe software solves it. Type 2s give great interviews and never buy.
2. **Budget direction wrong:** the software-adjacent area he mentioned positively (back-office admin / incident reporting) is owned by Mark43, Axon, Tyler Technologies, Motorola. A new entrant needs a wedge that didn't surface in this conversation.
3. **Real pain (recruitment) is owned by a separate department.** Marley did not ask for the intro. The conversation effectively ended one introduction away from the actual problem owner.

This is now the second vertical in two days where the workflow has produced high-quality information and zero advancement. The pattern is starting to look less like vertical-specific bad luck and more like a vertical-selection issue: Marley is picking domains where she has authentic personal context (boater on Tonka, district connections through family) but where the customer's actual procurement behavior doesn't support software entry.
