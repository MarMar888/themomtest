# Conversation Summary — Anelise Peterson, Minnetonka Public Schools
**Date:** 2026-06-11 | **Format:** Video call | **Interviewer:** Marley

---

## Who She Is

Anelise Peterson — district-level administrator at Minnetonka Public Schools, responsible for coordinating the attendance intervention system across buildings. Reports under Christine Breen (Executive Director of Students). Works closely with Matt Breen (Lead Data Scientist) on the EduClimber backend and with Annie Lamar Benson (Director of Health Services) and behavior strategists on the intervention side. 25 years in the district — started as a classroom teacher, moved into admin. This conversation was arranged through Matt Breen.

---

## What She Said

**On the current attendance process:**
The district has defined thresholds: 3 unexcused absences triggers a letter, then 7, 12, 17. Excused absences have a parallel track (7, 10, 17 days). State law requires reporting to the county at 7 unexcused days, but letters are meant to precede that. MDE defines chronic absence as 18+ days — the equivalent of skipping an entire year of education across a K–12 career.

Process has been inconsistent across buildings: some principals chose not to send letters when they'd already approved a vacation; others sent letters regardless. *"How can we help get them here if you're not sending letters to everybody across the board?"* Inconsistency = equity problem and accountability gap.

**On EduClimber and the letter-sending gap:**
EduClimber can generate smart form letters but cannot send them automatically. *"I have to do like three clicks to get the letter to be sent out."* On the receiving end, parents must enter a security code to open letters — and many don't. The platform shows delivered/received status, but *"a large population of the families we're sending it to may not open it."*

Further complication: the person typically executing the clicks is a paraprofessional — who can see all student data in EduClimber. *"For data privacy, that doesn't really fit either."* No clean role exists for who should own letter-sending.

To actually send letters, the district must exit EduClimber and go back to Skyward (the SIS), then manually address and send — through Skyward or by snail mail. The two systems don't integrate for this workflow.

**On the excused-absence loophole:**
The district is running into a growing problem: families excuse everything, so the intervention trigger never fires. *"There's a student that has missed 29 days, full days of school, not including partial days. They're all listed as excused — either family, vacation, illness."* The student has anxiety-driven school avoidance; parents are excusing each day individually. *"The intervention didn't happen early enough... it turned into this snowball effect."*

The district's response: at 17 excused absences, additional absences automatically become unexcused regardless of reason. After that, illness requires a doctor's note.

**On the new system being built for next year:**
The district is implementing a tiered threshold system with a 10%-of-instructional-time rule to distinguish fall absences from spring ones. Within EduClimber, they're building:
- Smart forms for letter tracking (weekly threshold reports → attendance team reviews → manual flag in EduClimber → live dashboard)
- Intervention tracking document: letters sent, parent meetings, all contact in one smart form per student
- Check-in/check-out form with attendance focus goal and student/parent attendance contracts

Training will be done by Anelise and Annie Lamar Benson. Matt Breen will configure the EduClimber backend over the summer. EduClimber is also launching a new attendance intervention module after July 1 — the district will need to personalize it.

**On scale of the problem:**
Worse at elementary than high school. *"There has been an increase in the elementary... that's why we started looking at the absenteeism."* High school has natural consequences (course-drop policy at 10 days) that elementary lacks. She didn't have a district-wide percentage on hand and was going to email Annie Lamar Benson during the call to get the number.

**On elementary half-day counting inaccuracy:**
Elementary schools only have two attendance blocks (morning/afternoon). A student leaving 10 minutes early = half-day absence. Same if they arrive late. *"We can't really capture that true information."* A student leaving 30 minutes early weekly for therapy accumulates half-day absences that look far worse than the actual time missed. The district is trying to pull from a more granular SIS data source to fix this, but it hasn't been implemented.

**On her wish:**
*"It would have been great if EduClimber could be like... they hit three, they hit seven, OK I check a box saying send it to parent, and it would go right out."* She also described wanting letters to include parent-facing resources: a short video, "here are five things you can try," data on the academic impact of absences.

---

## Key Signals

- Manual letter-sending is the core operational pain: multiple people, multiple systems, no automation, data privacy conflict around who can execute it
- The equity/consistency problem (principal discretion) is a named pain — shows up in fairness concerns and in accountability gaps when cases escalate to county
- The district is actively building a workaround system inside EduClimber this summer — not shopping for an external tool
- The excused-absence loophole is growing — school avoidance cases are rising at the elementary level and bypassing the intervention system entirely
- Elementary attendance data is structurally inaccurate (half-day rounding) — decisions are being made on imprecise inputs

## Bad Data

- Anelise wished for video content in letters and parent-facing resources — aspirational, not committed
- Marley described this as "an incredible opportunity" mid-conversation — his opinion, not customer data

---

## The Verdict

The pain is real and specific. Two independent voices from the same district (Breen + Peterson) have now described the same gap — manual, inconsistent, multi-system letter-sending — in matching detail. The workarounds confirm the problem is painful enough that they're investing significant administrative time to manage it. But the district's stated plan is to solve this inside EduClimber, not by purchasing something external. The strategic question is whether there's a wedge EduClimber can't fill: the Skyward integration gap, the letter automation layer, the parent communication problem. That question needs to be tested with other districts before assuming Minnetonka is the buyer.
