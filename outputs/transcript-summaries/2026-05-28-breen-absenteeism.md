# Conversation Summary — Matt Breen, Minnetonka Schools
**Date:** May 28–29, 2026 | **Format:** Text chain (ongoing) | **Interviewer:** Marley

---

## Who He Is

Matt Breen — Lead Data Scientist, Minnetonka School District. Former high school stats teacher (related stats to sports). Minnetonka is a well-resourced suburban district. His assistant aggregates raw data files. He uses eduCLIMBER as his primary platform for student performance analysis.

---

## What He Said

**On data and performance analysis:**
He primarily uses eduCLIMBER, pulling in demographic info as needed. Raw data files are aggregated separately by his assistant. He described the setup as functional but pointed to attendance as the area with the most active process problems.

**On the attendance intervention workflow:**
The current process is more manual review than automated alerting — "an 'alert' is still an overstatement." At a certain number of absences, buildings send hard copy letters to parents. Follow-up letters continue until truancy needs to be filed with the county. His exact words: "it has too many human touch points." Part of the problem is their old Student Information System.

**On whether the letters work:**
"Can't say for certain how effective the letters are, but in general, I would say they're not terribly effective until there is some teeth behind them. And the teeth doesn't really come until the County would have to be involved."

A new development: high school students can now be dropped from classes for attendance — a policy change that creates a new consequence point before county involvement.

**On tools in use:**
- Skyward: primary Student Information System
- eduCLIMBER: data analysis platform
- Frontline: used for job postings (HR) only — not for attendance

**On whether the district has bought something specifically for attendance:**
"Not that I'm aware of." No dedicated attendance intervention tool has been purchased. The problem is being managed through general-purpose platforms not designed for it.

**On data and family communication:**
"I also think we need to do a better job of informing families of the impact of absences on student growth. I could clearly find that to be the case in the data." He has done aggregate analysis (building-level trends, quarterly at the high school) but has never specifically looked at outcomes for students who received letters. The loop between intervention and outcome has never been closed.

**Awaiting response:**
Asked who is in charge of follow-up and intervention at the district or building level.

---

## Key Signals

- No dedicated solution purchased for attendance intervention — confirmed gap
- Letters are legally required compliance (MN Statute: 3 unexcused absences triggers notification) but operationally ineffective
- The data to connect absences to growth outcomes exists in eduCLIMBER — it's not being used to communicate with families
- Matt himself identified the gap: families don't know what absences are doing to their child's trajectory
- Nobody has ever evaluated whether the letter intervention actually works for the students who received them
- New class-drop policy at HS creates a new intervention trigger that doesn't yet have a process behind it
