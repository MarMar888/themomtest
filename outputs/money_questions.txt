MONEYBALL QUESTIONS — High-Signal Questions to Ask Next Time
Based on: Mom Test framework + gaps from May 11 transcripts
======================================================================

These are questions that, if answered well, give us real data — specific past stories,
actual spending, revealed behavior. NOT opinions. NOT hypotheticals.

OPERATIONS & INVENTORY PAIN
-----------------------------
1. "Walk me through what happened the last time you ran out of a popular model before the end of season."
   Why: Forces a specific story. Reveals whether inventory mismatch is a real recurring pain or just mild friction.

2. "What does your process look like right now for tracking what's selling fast vs. sitting on the floor?"
   Why: Reveals current workflow and workarounds. If the answer is "we kind of just watch it" — that's a real gap.

3. "Have you ever tried any software or tools to help manage inventory or forecasting? What happened?"
   Why: The "have they searched" test. If no → problem probably isn't painful enough. If yes → learn what failed and why.

4. "What's the worst inventory mistake that happened in the last year or two — over-ordered, under-ordered, wrong mix?"
   Why: Past-specific, emotional. Reveals actual cost of the problem.

5. "When you placed your January order, how did you decide what quantities to order? Walk me through it."
   Why: Exposes the actual decision process. Reveals where judgment calls happen and where they're flying blind.


CUSTOMER & SALES DYNAMICS
---------------------------
6. "Tell me about the last time a customer walked out because you didn't have what they were looking for."
   Why: Puts a real story behind lost sales. Reveals frequency and emotional weight of the problem.

7. "What do customers get frustrated about most when they come in? Give me the last specific example you can remember."
   Why: Past-specific, surfaces real pain points from the customer side that the shop owner has to manage.

8. "You mentioned people come in not knowing what they want — walk me through what that looks like on a busy Saturday."
   Why: Anchors the general statement to a real moment. Reveals the actual cost in time and conversion rate.


BUSINESS MODEL & COSTS
------------------------
9. "What's the single thing about running this shop that costs the most in time or money that you didn't expect when you started?"
   Why: Open-ended and past-oriented. Surfaces surprises — the real pain is often not what you assumed.

10. "When you hit that net 180 deadline in late summer and you haven't sold through — what actually happens? Walk me through a real example."
    Why: This is the cash flow crisis moment. Reveals severity of the end-of-season problem and what they do about it.

11. "How much time does the owner spend on things that aren't biking — admin, ordering, accounting, staffing? Is that a sore spot?"
    Why: Probes whether operational overhead is a real pain the owner feels, or just normal cost of doing business.


DECISION-MAKING STRUCTURE
--------------------------
12. "If you were going to try a new tool or process here, who would have to sign off on that?"
    Why: Reveals the actual decision-maker. If it's the regional director and we've been talking to floor staff, we're in the wrong conversation.

13. "Has the owner ever tried to buy something — software, a system, a service — to solve an operations problem and had it not work out? What happened?"
    Why: Reveals prior purchase behavior. One of the strongest signals of willingness to pay.


COMMITMENT-CLOSING QUESTIONS (ask at the end of every conversation)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
14. "Is there anyone else you'd recommend we talk to who deals with this stuff more directly than you do?"
    Why: Intro request = commitment signal. If they enthusiastically intro you, the conversation was real. If they deflect, it probably wasn't.

15. "Would it be useful to show you what we're thinking about building and get your honest reaction? We could come back next week."
    Why: Asks for time commitment. If they say yes → real interest. "Let us know when you have something" → zombie.


THE DESTROY-YOUR-BUSINESS QUESTION (ask this every single conversation)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
16. "Is this actually a problem that bothers you enough that you'd spend money to fix it — or is it just something you've accepted as part of the job?"
    Why: Forces honest self-assessment. The answer "honestly, we've just kind of accepted it" is the most valuable signal you can get. It means the problem exists but the market isn't there — before you build anything.


NOTES ON HOW TO USE THESE
--------------------------
- Pick 3 before every conversation. Know what you're trying to learn.
- Don't fire them all in one conversation. Let the answers guide you to the next question.
- When they give a general answer, slow down: "Can you give me a specific example of when that happened?"
- The goal is never to validate your idea. The goal is to find out if this problem is real, frequent, and painful enough that someone would pay to fix it.
