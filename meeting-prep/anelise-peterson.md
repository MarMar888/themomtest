## Questions (in order)

Start with the process walkthrough — this is the safest opener and will naturally surface everything else.

**Process**
- "Walk me through what actually happens when a student hits the threshold. What's the first step, and who does it?"
- "How do you know when a letter has gone out? Is that tracked somewhere, or is it more manual?"
- "What does follow-up look like after a letter goes out?"

**Pain and Friction**
- "What's the hardest part of managing this?"
- "Has anything about the process changed in the last year or two?" *(the new class-drop policy at HS — does she know? Is there a process for it yet?)*

**Prior Attempts**
- "Have you ever tried to figure out whether the letters made a difference?" *(tests Matt's signal — let her say it herself)*

**Urgency**
- "What would have to be true for you to feel like the intervention is actually working?"
- "Is this something you're actively trying to improve, or more of a 'this is just how it works' situation?"

**Destroy-Your-Business Question** *(ask this — it's the one you're afraid to ask)*
- "Is the absenteeism problem actually something buildings feel urgency around, or is it more of a compliance checkbox at this point?"

## What NOT to Do

- Don't mention Matt said the letters don't work — let her say it herself
- Don't pitch anything — she agreed to 30 minutes, not a demo
- Don't double-question (two questions in one turn)
- Don't accept fluff — if she says "oh yeah it's a real challenge," ask: "Can you walk me through the last time that was a problem?"
- Don't accept vague frequency language — "sometimes" → "how often is that, roughly?"

---

## Commitment Ask

End with a specific ask. Pick one:
- Intro to whoever decides what tools or processes get adopted (principal, curriculum director, budget holder)
- Intro to the other person who oversees the process
- Permission to follow up with a more structured conversation after you've done more research

Do NOT end with "I'll send you more info when it's ready." That is a zombie close.

---

## Signals to Listen For

**Strong signals**
- Specific recent story with emotion ("last month we had a family where...")
- Workarounds she's already built (spreadsheet, manual tracker, extra staff time)
- Prior spending — has the district piloted or evaluated any tool for this?
- She volunteers intros to others with the same problem

**Weak signals (don't count these)**
- "Yeah, it's definitely a challenge" with no story behind it
- Future-tense: "I'd love something that could do X"
- Enthusiasm without any specificity

---

## Lessons from Past Mistakes (things_to_avoid.txt)

The following patterns have burned us before. All are likely on this call.

**Accepting the wishlist without anchoring it**
She will describe what she wishes the process could do. Every "it would be great if..." needs a follow-up: "Have you ever tried something like that? What happened?" Notes and a nod is not enough.

**Letting prior spending pass**
Matt said no dedicated tool purchased — but that's from the data side. If Anelise mentions anything the district has ever piloted, evaluated, or paid for, stop and go all the way in: "How much? Did it work? Why did it stop?" Prior spending is the only hard willingness-to-pay signal available. Letting it pass is the worst miss you can make.

**No commitment ask**
The most consistent failure across every conversation logged. Decide before the call which ask you're making — intro to the other process owner, intro to the budget/decision-maker, or a follow-up with a specific agenda — and have the sentence ready so you don't fumble when the 30 minutes runs out.

**Accepting vague frequency language**
"We deal with it a lot" is not data. → "How often is that, roughly — weekly, monthly?" This is a fast-moving virtual call. Fluff will come fast and feel like signal.

**Skipping the have-they-searched test**
When she mentions the current process is inadequate, that's the moment to ask: "Have you ever looked for tools or approaches specifically for this? What stopped you?" Missing this skips the strongest urgency signal in the conversation.
